# Economic Growth and Environmental Sustainability in Selected OECD Countries

This project examines economic growth and environmental sustainability across 10 selected OECD countries from 2011 to 2023 using World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI) data.

The analysis looks at whether economic growth has taken place alongside changes in environmental conditions, particularly CO₂ emissions and forest coverage.

## Indicators Used

- GDP per capita (constant 2015 US$)
- CO₂ emissions excluding LULUCF per capita
- Forest area (% of land area)

## Countries Covered

Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, South Korea, Spain, United Kingdom and United States.

## Analysis

The project uses R for:

- Country-wise rankings
- Percentage change from 2011 to 2023
- Trend analysis
- Cross-country average comparison
- GDP–CO₂ decoupling analysis
- Scatter plots
- Pearson correlation
- Simple linear regression

## Key Findings

GDP per capita increased across all selected countries during the period, while CO₂ emissions per capita declined. Forest area remained relatively stable in most countries.

The decoupling analysis showed that economic growth occurred alongside falling per-capita CO₂ emissions. GDP and CO₂ emissions also showed a moderate positive correlation when all country-year observations were considered together.

## Tools Used

R, R Markdown, dplyr, tidyr, ggplot2, readxl

## Data Source

World Bank – World Development Indicators (WDI)

## Note

The correlation and regression results represent statistical associations and should not be interpreted as causal relationships.
